A decision markdown file that fails to parse during reconcile was already collected into ReconcileReport.malformed and already warned about — it was never silent. What was missing was the other half of graphify's lesson: the report was one line per file and unbounded, so a corrupt checkout buried every other finding under a wall of near-identical lines.

Decided: cap the human rendering at five entries and state the remainder rather than dropping it. The cap and the omission line live in one helper (src/cli/malformed-files.ts) that both doctor and sync call, so a caller cannot take the cap and forget the second half — a silent truncation is the failure mode this is built to prevent.

reconcile.ts was deliberately not touched: the aggregate is a presentation concern, and the report keeps carrying every entry.

The escape hatch is named in the omission line itself: cortex sync --json prints the reconcile report whole and stays uncapped, so exactly one complete, machine-readable list always exists. That is also why the cap applies to doctor --json — DoctorReport builds its checks once and both renderers walk the same array — rather than threading a flag down into every check.

Five was chosen because doctor emits roughly a dozen checks in a healthy repo: enough entries to see whether the failures share a cause (one directory, one reason, one bad merge) while the remainder count stays the headline.
