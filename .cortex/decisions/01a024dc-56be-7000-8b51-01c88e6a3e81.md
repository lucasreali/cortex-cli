The file was the pre-implementation plan for shipping a binary, and it was still shipped as documentation long after the work landed: future tense throughout, a step telling the reader to create `.github/` because it "does not exist yet", a warning that the repository might be misnamed `cortext-cli`, and a musl probe written the opposite way round from the one in `install.sh`. Every one of those reads as current fact to someone who opens `docs/`.

Rewritten as a description of the shipped pipeline, organised by the four pieces that carry it — `scripts/` builds and packages, `install.sh` brings the first binary in, `src/release/` replaces one already there, `.github/workflows/` runs it from a tag — and keeping only the reasoning a reader cannot recover from the code: why the build stays on the `Bun.build` JS API, why the suffix is baked in at compile time, why each target keeps its own sourcemap, why install.sh stages inside the install directory, why the daemon socket is keyed by model rather than version.

The roadmap survives as a closing "Not shipped" section, which is where a plan belongs once the rest of the document is fact.

`tests/docs/distribution.test.ts` pins the one list the prose cannot be allowed to drift on: the targets it names must be exactly `RELEASE_SUFFIXES`, and the asset count it claims must be two per target plus checksums.txt.
